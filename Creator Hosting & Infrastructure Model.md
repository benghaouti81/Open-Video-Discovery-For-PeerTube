Creator Hosting & Infrastructure Model

Purpose

A decentralized video ecosystem should not require every creator to operate their own server.

Creators may have valuable content and an audience without having the infrastructure, bandwidth, technical knowledge, or resources required to run a PeerTube instance.

This project therefore supports a flexible hosting ecosystem in which creators can either operate their own infrastructure or work with independent hosting providers and creator networks.

The principle is simple:

«Hosting should be a choice, not a barrier to participation.»

---

1. Creator-Operated Hosting

Creators and organizations may operate their own PeerTube instances.

They can manage their own:

- Infrastructure
- Storage and bandwidth
- Channel administration
- Content policies
- Federation settings
- Commercial relationships

This provides the highest level of direct control for creators who have the necessary resources.

---

2. Independent Hosting Providers

Creators who do not want to operate their own infrastructure may use an independent hosting provider.

A hosting provider can operate PeerTube infrastructure for one creator or many creators and may provide:

- Server infrastructure
- Storage
- Bandwidth
- Backups
- Maintenance
- Monitoring
- Technical support

Providers may range from small community-operated servers to professional infrastructure companies with substantial storage and bandwidth.

They do not need to offer identical services or operate at the same scale.

---

3. Paid Hosting

Under a paid hosting arrangement, the creator pays the provider for the infrastructure and services provided.

The creator may independently manage:

- Advertising
- Sponsorships
- Advertiser relationships
- Commercial agreements

The provider's primary role is to provide infrastructure and related services.

A creator may also choose to use additional commercial services offered by the provider if both parties agree.

---

4. Sponsored & Revenue-Share Hosting

A provider may also offer free or subsidized hosting to creators.

Instead of charging the creator directly, the provider may recover its costs through an agreed share of the creator's commercial revenue.

In this model, the provider may take responsibility for the commercial side of the creator's channel, including:

- Finding advertisers
- Maintaining advertiser relationships
- Negotiating campaigns
- Managing sponsorships
- Handling commercial administration
- Collecting advertising or sponsorship revenue

The creator and provider agree privately on the commercial terms and revenue-sharing arrangement.

For example:

             Creator
                │
        Content & Channel
                │
                ▼
       Hosting / Network
          │           │
          │           └── Advertising & Sponsorship
          │
          └── Infrastructure
                │
                ▼
         Agreed Revenue Share

The project does not prescribe a particular percentage or commercial arrangement.

---

5. Creator Networks

Some organizations may go beyond hosting and operate as Creator Networks.

A creator network may combine:

- Hosting infrastructure
- Technical support
- Advertising
- Sponsorship management
- Commercial representation
- Production services
- Support for multiple creators

A creator network may therefore act as both a hosting provider and a commercial partner.

The distinction is useful:

Hosting Provider
Primarily provides infrastructure and technical services.

Creator Network
May provide infrastructure together with commercial, administrative, or production services.

One organization may perform both roles.

---

6. Different Models, Same Ecosystem

Creators may choose different arrangements depending on their circumstances.

Model| Infrastructure| Advertising & Sponsorship
Self-hosted| Creator| Creator
Paid hosting| Provider| Creator or separately agreed
Sponsored hosting| Provider| Provider and/or creator, according to agreement
Creator network| Network| Network, creator, or shared arrangement

There is no requirement for every creator to follow the same model.

The ecosystem should allow different organizations to offer different services and compete or cooperate according to their own terms.

---

7. Ownership and Portability

Using a hosting provider should not automatically mean giving up ownership of the creator's work.

Hosting agreements should clearly define:

- Content ownership
- Channel ownership
- Administrative rights
- Advertising and sponsorship rights
- Revenue-sharing terms
- Access to creator data
- What happens when the agreement ends

Where technically and legally possible, creators should have a practical path to migrate their content and channel information to another compatible provider.

Open standards and portable data are therefore important principles.

The objective is to provide infrastructure without creating unnecessary lock-in.

---

8. Commercial Relationships

Advertising and sponsorship arrangements may take different forms.

A creator may work directly with advertisers, or may authorize a hosting provider or creator network to manage those relationships on their behalf.

In either case, the commercial arrangement belongs to the parties involved.

The Open Video Discovery project does not set advertising prices, revenue-sharing percentages, or commercial contracts between creators, providers, networks, and advertisers.

These matters remain subject to their individual agreements and applicable laws.

---

9. Infrastructure and Sustainability

Video hosting has real operational costs, including storage, bandwidth, servers, maintenance, backups, connectivity, and technical support.

A sustainable ecosystem therefore needs different ways to cover these costs.

Possible models include:

- Paid hosting
- Sponsored hosting
- Revenue sharing
- Community-supported infrastructure
- Creator networks
- Institutional or organizational hosting

No single model is assumed to be suitable for everyone.

---

10. Guiding Principle

The project supports an ecosystem in which the following functions can remain independent:

Creation → Hosting → Commercial Services → Discovery

A creator may control all of these functions, or delegate some of them to specialized organizations.

This allows creators with different resources and needs to participate without requiring a single company to control the entire process.

The goal is not to replace one centralized platform with another.

It is to make room for an open ecosystem of creators, infrastructure providers, creator networks, and other independent participants.
